    L'Affrontement · live Elo race for the Red Bull Wings Cup

    To promote the Red Bull Wings Cup at ENSEEIHT, I turned an FC 27 tournament into a duel between the school's two student lists, Chau7 and 7xAppeal: nearly 200 students, 173 of them registered to play.

    I built the whole system: an n8n automation collects the match results, updates an Elo rating for every player, and a live TV-style page shows the two lists' scores, the gap between them and the latest results, refreshed automatically every five minutes.

    • Fair scoring: each player starts at 1,500 points (K-factor 32), and a list's score is the sum of its players' gains and losses, plus a small bonus for the list with the highest registration rate
    • Privacy: students' names never appear publicly; they are replaced by anonymous identifiers
    • Engagement: a rivalry the whole school could follow on screen, which kept people talking about the Wings Cup

    Inbox decision dashboard · Red Bull

    The problem. My manager covers field marketing for the whole South-West. Her inbox mixes event requests from students, partnership requests, event logistics and spam. For each request she had to check the date, find two available Student Marketeers, answer the organiser and write the brief, all by hand.

    What I built. On my own time, as a personal prototype: a dashboard that reads at a glance and turns every email into a decision.

    • One column per type of request, one line per email: the deadline (urgent ones in red), the event and the organisation
    • One decision: Yes, Maybe or No. “Yes” sends the answer to the organiser and the brief to the team; “Maybe” asks exactly for the missing information; “No” sends a polite refusal
    • The real team and the real schedule: it suggests the pair of Student Marketeers already on shift that day, in the right area
    • It learns from past events to suggest the right kind of activation
    • A weekly view of shifts, accepted events and briefs still to send, plus the regular partners who have not been in touch yet this year

    Guardrails. Nothing is sent without her click. The brand's rules are built in. Emails stay internal: they are never sent to an external AI, and automatic sorting will run on the AI tools approved by the company.

    Next steps. Connect the real Outlook inbox, then switch on sorting with the company's AI (Microsoft Copilot or Azure).

    Aircraft on-board electrical network · S6

    On new aircraft, hydraulic flight-control circuits are being replaced by electrical ones to save weight. Electricity becomes the main energy carrier, and manufacturers chose a 270 V DC network to connect loads and storage easily. We designed and simulated that network in PLECS.

    The chain we studied

    • Generator: a three-phase alternator driven by the engine at 12,000 rpm (400 Hz), tested at no load and on a linear load
    • Rectifier and filter: a diode rectifier and LC filter that create the 270 V DC bus, studied under load
    • Battery back-up: a DC/DC converter with its own control loop to exchange energy between the bus and the battery
    • Actuators: voltage inverters supplying the flight-control motors, a DC motor and an induction motor, whose power changes quickly with each flight phase

    The labs behind it

    • Voltage inverter: full-wave versus modulated (PWM) control, energy transfer and FFT of the input current
    • Three-phase rectifiers: P3 and PD3 with diodes, then PD3 with thyristors and a firing-angle control built from the line voltages
    • Electrical machines: study of the synchronous and asynchronous (induction) machine

    What it gave me: thinking about a system as a chain where every block changes the next one, and checking a model against theory (Fourier series, power balance) before trusting a simulation.
